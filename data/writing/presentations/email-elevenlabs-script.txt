In Part 2, you may write an email to a friend, or a formal email to someone you don't know. The big difference is the register: the tone and the expressions you choose. In these examples, you would like to join a summer English course in July. You write to your friend Tom, who told you about it, and to the language school. Here is the same message written twice.
<break time="1.0s" />
First, the opening. To a friend: Hi Tom, it was great to get your message! To the school: Dear Sir or Madam, I am writing to enquire about your summer English course in July.
<break time="1.0s" />
Next, the main part. To a friend: Why don't we go together? Let me know what dates you're thinking about. To the school: Could you please tell me whether there are still places available? I would also like to know if accommodation is included.
<break time="1.0s" />
Finally, the closing. To a friend: Can't wait to catch up! Take care, Alex. To the school: I look forward to hearing from you. Yours faithfully, Alex Martin.
<break time="1.0s" />
Remember: after Dear Sir or Madam, end with Yours faithfully. In a formal email, avoid contractions and exclamation marks.

When you are ready, you can try these expressions on the cards.
