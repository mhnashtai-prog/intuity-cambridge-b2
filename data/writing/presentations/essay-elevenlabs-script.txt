A B2 First essay is a formal piece of writing, between one hundred and forty and one hundred and ninety words, written for your teacher. It answers a question, discusses both sides, and gives your own opinion. In these examples, the question is: some people say tourism does more harm than good. Do you agree?

An essay usually begins by setting the scene with a general statement. The next sentence says what the essay will do. Here are three examples.
<break time="1.0s" />
There has been a growing recognition that tourism can damage the places people love to visit. Consequently, I will explain why I disagree with this statement.
<break time="1.0s" />
It is a well-established fact that tourism is one of the largest industries in the world. Hence, I would like to present my views on whether it does more harm than good.
<break time="1.0s" />
For a more extended version, the two parts can be combined into one sentence. Over the past few years, there has been a significant increase in tourism, which is why it is important to examine the arguments both for and against it.
<break time="1.5s" />
The body of an essay presents the arguments, one in each paragraph, using the notes in the question. Each paragraph opens with an expression that introduces the argument. A linker then supports it with a reason or an example. Here are three examples.
<break time="1.0s" />
One strong argument in favour of tourism is that it creates jobs for millions of people. This is because hotels, restaurants and shops all need staff.
<break time="1.0s" />
On the other hand, a key argument against tourism is that it negatively affects the environment. For example, popular beaches are often left full of rubbish.
<break time="1.0s" />
For a more extended version, an argument and its opposite can share one sentence. This is also a good place for your own idea. It is often claimed that tourism helps to protect local traditions, although critics argue that it can also turn them into a show for visitors.
<break time="1.5s" />
The final paragraph gives your opinion clearly. It sums up both sides, says which is stronger, and does not introduce anything new. Here are three examples.
<break time="1.0s" />
On balance, it appears that tourism brings more advantages than disadvantages. However, it must be managed carefully.
<break time="1.0s" />
In conclusion, although there are strong arguments on both sides, I firmly believe that tourism does more good than harm.
<break time="1.0s" />
For a more extended version, the conclusion can add a condition. To sum up, while tourism has both supporters and critics, the evidence strongly supports it, provided that visitors respect the places they explore.

When you are ready, you can try these expressions on the cards.
