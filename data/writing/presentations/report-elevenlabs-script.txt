A B2 First report is a formal text, between one hundred and forty and one hundred and ninety words, usually written for a teacher, a manager or a club. It is divided into sections with clear headings, presents what you found out, and ends with recommendations. In these examples, the head teacher wants to improve the school canteen.

A report usually begins by saying what it is for. The next sentence says where the information comes from. Here are three examples.
<break time="1.0s" />
The purpose of this report is to examine how students feel about the school canteen. The information in this report was collected through a survey among two hundred students.
<break time="1.0s" />
This report aims to provide a summary of students' views on the canteen. The findings in this report are drawn from interviews and feedback during lunch breaks last month.
<break time="1.0s" />
For a more extended version, the two parts can be combined into one sentence. The main objective of this report is to assess the school canteen, based on a questionnaire completed by students in every year group.
<break time="1.5s" />
The middle sections present the findings, often under headings such as positive points and problems. Each section opens with an expression that sums up what people said. A reason or a detail then supports it. Here are three examples.
<break time="1.0s" />
By far the best aspect of the canteen is that meals are freshly cooked every day. Many students referred to the pasta as the best part of the week.
<break time="1.0s" />
One major concern expressed by students is the length of the queues. This is mainly due to the fact that only one counter is open at lunchtime.
<break time="1.0s" />
For a more extended version, the finding and the reason can share one sentence. It was generally felt that the menu is too repetitive, considering that the same dishes appear every week.
<break time="1.5s" />
The final section makes recommendations and draws a conclusion. It suggests clear, practical changes and does not introduce new findings. Here are three examples.
<break time="1.0s" />
In order to reduce the queues, I would strongly recommend opening a second counter. This would save students at least ten minutes every day.
<break time="1.0s" />
To sum up, the findings clearly show that students value the canteen, but they would like more variety.
<break time="1.0s" />
For a more extended version, the conclusion and the recommendation can share one sentence. Taking everything into account, it seems clear that the canteen is popular, but one key recommendation would be to offer a wider choice of dishes.

When you are ready, you can try these expressions on the cards.
